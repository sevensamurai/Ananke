The Signal Behind Learning

Happened and mattered are not the same thing — combining them produces a system that learns the routine and forgets the remarkable. Biological cognition decomposes the learning event into three orthogonal signals.

Signal
Question it answers
Role in learning
Valence
Was the outcome good or bad?
Shapes what surfaces in future recall — priority, not truth
Intensity
How much did it matter?
Amplifies or suppresses the priority signal
Surprise
How unexpected was it?
Determines the learning rate — proportional to prediction error
Rescorla-Wagner · 1972
Prediction Error Learning
Learning rate proportional to prediction error. If an outcome matches expectation, the update is small. Surprising outcomes drive significant revision. The basis of temporal-difference learning in modern reinforcement learning.
Damasio · 1994
Somatic Marker Hypothesis
The valence trace of past outcomes shapes future decisions faster than deliberate reasoning — not by computing, but by leaving a mark that surfaces automatically during recall.

Aether borrows the architecture, not the phenomenology.
